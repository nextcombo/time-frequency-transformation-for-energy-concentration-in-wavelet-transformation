# Time-Frequency Transformation for Energy Concentration in Wavelet Transformation for Transient Signal Analysis [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction
As signal detection technology advances, there are increasingly numerous instances of transient signals available for practical applications. These include fault diagnosis in rotating motors, ECG (Electrocardiogram) data processing, and GW (Gravitational Wave) signal detection [1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]. Such signals can typically be precisely modeled as the superposition of amplitude modulation and frequency modulation (AM-FM) modes, namely multicomponent signals [7]. Since the variation patterns of transient signals are often represented by frequency variables, group delay (GD) defined as the derivative of phase to frequency can more effectively reveal the transient characteristics [8], [9]. Therefore, accurately characterizing the GD of transient signals is of significant importance in practical applications. In fact, transient signals are a typical type of non-stationary signal with frequency variation characteristics. The classical Fourier transform (FT) over time is difficult to reflect the characteristic changes of such signals. Time-frequency (TF) analysis (TFA) is one of the effective strategies for local description of the TF variations of non-stationary signals. Typical linear TFA methods, such as Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) and Wavelet Transform (WT), extend one-dimensional time series signals to two-dimensional (2-D) TF planes to observe the complex structure of the signal. They can obtain both time and frequency information simultaneously through the temporal conversion and frequency modulation of the Heisenberg box [10], [11] and the observation window. Additionally, to compensate for the flexibility of window angles in linear TFA, a special linear transformation (CT) was proposed to improve the TF representation (TFR) of chirped signals by projecting the signal onto a series of chirps [12], [13]. However, due to the nature of linear approximation, CT is not suitable for analyzing nonlinear FM signals. To characterize nonlinear FM signals, Yang et al. proposed a parameterized TFA method called Generalized Parameterized TF Transform (GPTFT) by changing the kernel function in CT [14]. Moreover, to accurately describe the variation patterns of transient signals, Yang et al. also introduced a frequency domain GPTFT as a dual version of GPTFT [15]. However, this parameter matching strategy can only accurately describe a single component signal or multiple components with the same vibration law. Furthermore, linear TFA methods always suffer from the limitation of low energy concentration in the TF plane known as the Heisenberg uncertainty principle [16], [17].
Many advanced schemes have been developed to improve the resolution of classical TFA methods, either exclusively in the frequency domain (such as Empirical Wavelet Transform (EWT)) or in the time-frequency plane (such as Reweighted Method (RM)). Since the scaling properties of wavelet basis functions are equivalent to filters with different scales, EWT has become a valuable data-driven solution for adaptively decomposing non-stationary multicomponent signals into various Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMF) and Residual Functions [18]. It is possible to obtain the instantaneous frequencies (IF) of IMFs through further application of Hilbert transform. However, due to the limitations of narrowband conditions, EWT often overdecomposes when processing broadband transient signals.
The post-processing scheme for linear TFA has recently become a popular research area. The Reweighted Method (RM), as a focus of post-processing schemes, was proposed in [19] and developed in [20]. The main steps of RM involve exploring the location with maximum energy, called Ridges [21], [22], and accumulating TF energy onto these ridges through bidirectional reweighting operators, thus accurately describing the TF characteristics of non-stationary multicomponent signals. Unfortunately, the loss of phase information during the reweighting process prevents RM from retrieving the inherent defects of the original signal.
To bridge this gap, [23] introduced a special phase-based RM, namely Synchronous Squeezing Transform (SST). It retains the original linear transformation's phase information by accumulating TF coefficients along the frequency direction instead of re-distributing energy. The advantages of SST in signal reconstruction and resolution improvement have led to the development of various SST-based schemes to enhance its flexibility [24], [25], [26], [27], [28]. Inspired by SST, [29] proposed the Synchronous Extraction Transform (SET) scheme, which only retains the Ridge Energy to achieve a TFR with less noise interference. In [30], a method that uses TF peak filtering to guide the SET extraction process improved the energy concentration of SET. [23, 31] and [32] further provided theoretical support for both SST and SET families, ensuring their effectiveness in practical applications. However, SET and SST based on the weak FM signal assumption provide poor TF resolution for transient signals with steep IFs.
[33], [34], [35], [36] and [37] respectively present Higher-Order Reweighting (HSST) and Multi-SST (MSST), enhancing the applicability of SST and SET families. By considering the high-order Taylor expansion of signal phases, coefficients of SST or SET can be compressed or extracted, allowing HSST and HSET to yield IF candidates closer to the actual IF. However, the higher-order approximation process may introduce heavier computational burdens. MSST employs a fixed-point iterative strategy to reduce the error range between the actual IF and its candidate IF. At the same time, in MSST, only a second-order approximation model is needed for phase, greatly reducing computational load. However, these frequency redistribution methods are only applicable to signals with single-valued time-dependent functions. Therefore, transient signals with multi-valued IFs within the SST and SET frameworks are difficult to accurately describe [9] and [10].
In recent years, Time-Reweighted SST (TSST) technology has achieved SST duality by considering the frequency domain signal model and introducing GD with single-valued frequency-dependent functions [38]. However, TSST is limited to weakly frequency-varying signals. Moreover, TSST is embedded within the STFT framework. Although STFT can describe non-stationary signals, its observation window lacks adaptability. Therefore, STFT cannot optimize for specific frequency ranges. WT can flexibly select an appropriate window length to observe different frequency regions of the signal, which allows WT to better reveal the characteristics of transient signals with singularities [10, 39].
The purpose of this paper is to effectively characterize transient signals by introducing a new post-processing method and its improvements. A scheme called the Time-Frequency Reweighted SST (WTSST) is proposed to address transient signals. The detailed theoretical support for WTSST is further provided, and the GD error under strong frequency-varying signals is analyzed. Based on the analysis, an improved version called the Time-Frequency Reweighted MSST (WTMSST), which reduces the GD candidate residual terms of strong frequency-varying signals, is also proposed. This approach aims to approximate the actual GD of transient signals with reasonable accuracy.

Conclusion
This paper develops an effective tool for accurate extraction of transient events. The GD estimator first defines through using the centroid of the TF distribution of transient signals. Then, the energy-concentrated TFR is obtained by aggregating the TF coefficients of WT along the time direction on the GD estimator. The complete theoretical analysis of WTSST proves its feasibility for transient signals. In addition, the iterative collection of GD candidates will navigate towards more precise positions, making WTSST capable of describing strong frequency-varying transient signals more accurately. It has been applied in practical applications such as bearing fault diagnosis, heartbeat status detection, and GW detection, indicating that WTSST not only can precisely describe the characteristics of strong frequency-varying signals but also almost perfectly retrieves the original signal. This means that WTSST has potential applications in rich transient event scenarios, such as wheel tire blowout, collision detection, etc.
